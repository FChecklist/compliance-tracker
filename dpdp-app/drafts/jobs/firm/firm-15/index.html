DPDP job firm-15 · Part 3: Tell people & take consent · for companies, firms and NGOs

Publish a privacy policy on the website

Publish a privacy policy on the website. For a company, firm or NGO, this comes from SPDI Rules 2011 rule 4, DPDP Act 2023 §5, and DPDP Rules 2025 rule 3 — the SPDI part is in force TODAY, until 13 May 2027, and the DPDP part is in force from 13 May 2027. Usually done by Website firm — the company that built or runs your website; it counts as done when the document exists and its fingerprint, name and date are logged.

The law, by section and rule

  • Information Technology (Reasonable Security Practices and Procedures and Sensitive Personal Data or Information) Rules, 2011, rule 4 — a body corporate must publish a privacy policy on its website.
  • Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, section 5 — notice — what personal data is collected, for what purpose, how to exercise rights and how to complain to the Board.
  • Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, rule 3 — the notice a Data Fiduciary gives — itemised, standalone, understandable, with the means to exercise rights and complain. Both apply: the today's-law duty is what you can be held to now; the DPDP duty is what replaces it on 13 May 2027. Doing it once satisfies both.

In force today, or from 13 May 2027?

  • DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025 — in force from 13 May 2027

Do this now: the one page counts this job as required by today's law, and it is late the day it is missed — not on 13 May 2027.

Who usually does it

Website firm — the company that built or runs your website.

This job is answered by an outside firm — a Data Processor in the Act's words — not by your own staff. It is free for them; the data agreement is what makes them answer, and they are blocked until they sign.

If you have no website firm, the job can be marked "doesn't apply" and it drops out of the file.

What proof looks like

The document itself, kept by you where it belongs (here, published where people can find it). VERIDIAN records only its fingerprint, its name and the date — never the document — so a reviewer can later prove which version existed on which day.

Proof kind: doc. Proof mode: declare. VERIDIAN gives this job 10 days from the day the file starts; it is done once, not repeated — unless the answers change.

Why it matters

A notice tells people what you hold and why; consent is their yes. Without both, the processing has no lawful ground — and a consent that cannot be withdrawn as easily as it was given does not count.

This is today's law: under section 43A of the Information Technology Act, 2000, a body corporate that is negligent in keeping reasonable security practices can be ordered to compensate the person harmed.

From 13 May 2027, a person can take an unresolved grievance to the Data Protection Board of India, which can inquire and impose the penalties the Act's Schedule sets. This page does not quote figures — the Board decides each case, and no penalty order has been issued in India to date.
