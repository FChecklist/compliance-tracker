Why it matters

Three things every organisation should be able to say.

Most DPDP trouble is not bad intent. It is memory that walks out of the door.

1

People change.

When they leave, what they knew about your data leaves with them. Your file must not depend on one person's memory.

2

Know what you hold.

Know what personal data you hold, and who else holds it for you: vendors, group companies, processors.

3

Be able to prove it.

Keep a record that outlasts the person who set it up, with every answer dated and attributable.

How it works

From the law to a record, in four plain steps.

The law becomes jobs

Each obligation turns into a clear job, mapped to the section or rule it comes from.

Each job gets one owner

A named person is responsible. Owners, staff, vendors and group companies are all covered.

One email a week

Stakeholders answer from a single weekly email. No accounts, no passwords, nothing to install.

The record builds itself

Every answer is stored with its date and cannot be edited, giving you proof that stands on its own.
